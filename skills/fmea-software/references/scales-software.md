## Rules common to the three scales

One chain rates one end effect under one trigger; the anchors never require or reward a second row per effect or per trigger, a sub-element, or an element standing for the users hit, the trigger or a control layer. [skill-authored] Every anchor step is a function from a fact in the inputs to one value, and every step names the value an assumption-backed rating takes when the input is silent; no anchor asks the rater to confirm anything with anyone. [skill-authored] Evidence kind never caps or floors a value: an estimate may sit at 10 and an observed incident at 2. [skill-authored] Conversion never re-rates: every integer 1 to 10 is a legal authored value, authored values are copied and never snapped, remapped or reviewed against these anchors, and the evidence rules bind only ratings the skill writes. [skill-authored]

An update-mode re-rating carries forward: a stale row keeps its prior value on every factor the stale reason did not touch, `control-removed` re-reads D only, `element-changed` re-reads S from the end effect as the update rewrites it on the stale row, and O from that end effect and, where an edge changed, the SLA and limits, as they now stand, of the edge whose addition, removal or change marked the row stale, the same on the provider's rows and on a consumer row whose cause links into one of them; a consumer row's O rationale cites the provider's O and its evidence, recorded in the cause's `cited_o`, and where both rows are stale the provider's row is re-rated first; `function-changed` re-reads S, and `scales-version` re-reads all three. [skill-authored] Severity changes only if the design is modified; Occurrence changes with prevention controls; Detection changes with detection controls. [paraphrased:C090] A post-action re-rating in this skill moves a factor only through the matching kind of change. [skill-authored]

Dependency strength moves no rating; a loss-of-access end effect on a chain whose function belongs to an element that is the `to` of at least one edge, every such edge weak, is recorded as an open assumption on the edges' strength, owned by `user`, and Severity is rated on the end effect as written. [skill-authored] That check is the reviewer row `weak-edges-loss-of-access`, which `quality-and-lint.md` carries under the same id. [skill-authored]

## Severity, 1 to 10

**How Severity is read.** Severity is rated on `effects.end`, written as the worst credible end effect the row's causes and trigger produce without a second independent failure, and assessed — as Yuan et al. assess their catastrophic bar, with the system's existing availability mechanisms in place — after the row's existing evidenced compensating controls (`kind: compensating`, `status: existing`, `evidence.kind` other than `none`) have acted; planned and unevidenced controls do not reduce it. [adapted-from:C030] [skill-authored] A compensating control acts on a row only when its description names the row's failure mode, its trigger, or the condition the row's causes name, and the rationale quotes the matching words; a fallback stated for a dependency that does not answer acts on that dependency's unavailable row and not on a slow-answer or a retry row, and a worse case stands unless a control that acts on the row makes it incredible. [skill-authored]

Three facts set the value: effect class, blast radius, recoverability. [skill-authored] The class groups the six outage effect types Gunawi et al. name, carried in that study's own terms: full outage and failure of essential operations are **loss of access** (the function's stated outcome not delivered, or not within the budget its contract or SLA states); data loss is **loss of data**; security attack or breach is **loss of confidentiality**; data staleness or inconsistency is a **wrong result** (the function completes with an outcome the user or the business would not have accepted); performance glitch is a **slow or partial result** (the right outcome, late or to fewer requests). [adapted-from:C058] [skill-authored] Loss of access, of data and of confidentiality form the top class, wrong result sits below it and slow or partial below that; an effect that fits two classes takes the higher, and a run that cannot decide takes the higher. [skill-authored] A row whose `causes[]` names a sustaining-loop mechanism from the Occurrence list below, or whose mode is an overload, a saturation, a retry amplification or an unbounded queue, has loss of access as its worst credible class: with nothing acting, the function delivers no outcome to every request while the loop persists, so the class is never lowered to slow or partial for the time before saturation. [adapted-from:C034] [skill-authored]

Blast radius is measured over the function's users or requests while the mode is active: majority is more than half, subset is at least one external user and at most half, internal only is no external user (operators, finance, engineers, logs, cost). [skill-authored] Without a stated number the structural rule decides: a mode that hits every request reaching the function while it is active is majority; a mode that hits only requests or users meeting a further condition (a locale, a retried order, a session at expiry, a quote past its age, a captured token) is subset; a share stated in an incident record can raise the class and never lowers it, because a share measured over a window is diluted by the time the mode was not active. [skill-authored] A client, a tenant or a locale is a selecting condition only when the inputs name more than one; when the inputs name a single client, every request reaches the function through it and a mode that breaks that client is majority. [skill-authored]

Class and radius set the band: top class at majority is 9 to 10; top class at subset, and wrong result at majority, is 7 to 8; wrong result at subset, and slow or partial at majority, is 4 to 6; slow or partial at subset, and any class at internal only, is 2 to 3; no effect on any user, operator or data is 1. [skill-authored] Recoverability sets the step inside the band and never crosses one: **A** when an existing evidenced compensating control that acts on the row restores the state or ends the effect without a person, **H** when a stated procedure (a runbook, a support or finance process, a restart, a rollback, a fix the inputs name) restores or ends it, **N** when neither is in the inputs. [skill-authored] A loss of access ends with its trigger unless `causes[]` names a sustaining loop, so it is H by default and N when a loop is named; a slow or partial result always ends with its trigger, because the promotion rule above puts every row naming a loop in the loss-of-access class, so it is H by default; loss of data, a wrong result and an internal-only effect are N by default; a breach is always N. [adapted-from:C034] [skill-authored]

Duration never moves Severity; the time a mode is active belongs to Occurrence. [skill-authored] A breach is rated here like any other end effect whatever its cause, since D6 routes a row to the threat-model handoff by an adversarial cause and not by effect type, and it is never lowered for being a security row. [skill-authored] The evidence kind is `observed_incident` when an incident record on the row states the effect, `test_result` when a test or a drill produced it, otherwise `estimate`; the worst-credible rule governs a seeded row as it governs a design row, and an incident's stated share, duration or count of users moves the radius upward only. [skill-authored]

## Occurrence, 1 to 10

**How Occurrence is read.** Occurrence is anchored on exposure, not on a failure rate or a probability: before the system ships, a design-side analysis can say how much of the time an element sits where its trigger produces the mode and how the trigger arrives, and cannot honestly say how often the mode fails per unit of demand. [adapted-from:C032] [skill-authored] Two gates set the band and two modifiers set the step inside it. [skill-authored]

The first gate is the vulnerable state **V**, the state in which the trigger produces the mode, in Bronson et al.'s sense: **V-always** when the element is in it in normal operation (a quote with no maximum age, a check reading the wrong claim, a session with no binding to its device); **V-recurring** when it is entered only under a named recurring condition (a promotion window, a rollout, a batch, peak hours); **V-rare** when it is entered only by an unscheduled event that happens to the system (a failover, a dependency outage, a partition, a hardware fault); **V-excluded** when it is reachable only after a second independent failure or under a precondition the inputs state cannot hold. [adapted-from:C032] [skill-authored] For a mode entered when offered load exceeds a limit, V is decided by closeness to capacity in the corrected direction: a stated limit is the advertised capacity, the top of the vulnerable band, and the hidden capacity at which vulnerability begins lies below it and is not in the inputs; so V is recurring when the inputs state the limit and a normal load at or below half of it and name a recurring condition that exceeds it, rare when normal load is at or below half of it and only an unscheduled event exceeds it, and always otherwise, including whenever the limit or the normal load is not stated (the open-assumption default). [adapted-from:C032] [skill-authored]

The second gate is the trigger **T** named in `trigger`: **T-traffic** when it is ordinary use (any request, session or write while the element is in V); **T-scheduled** when it is an event the team or the business performs (a deploy, a rollout, a price change, a promotion, a batch, a configuration change); **T-rare** when it is an event that happens to the system (an outage, a failover, a partition, a replica failing readiness, an attacker's action); **T-none** when `trigger` is absent or empty, scored as T-rare and capped at 6. [adapted-from:C032] [adapted-from:C033] [skill-authored] A recurring condition is scored once, as V-recurring with T the traffic inside it or as V-always with T-scheduled, never both. [skill-authored] When the inputs name several initiating events, `trigger` names the one with the lowest gate score (traffic before scheduled before rare), the rationale lists the others, and no row is added per trigger. [skill-authored]

Gate scores are V-always 0, V-recurring 1, V-rare 2, T-traffic 0, T-scheduled 1, T-rare 2; the sum sets the band: 0 is 9 to 10, 1 is 7 to 8, 2 is 4 to 6, 3 or 4 is 2 to 3, and V-excluded is 1 whatever T is. [skill-authored] The step is the band's lowest value plus one per modifier present, never past the band's top. [skill-authored] The **deterministic** modifier is present when an incident or a test shows the mode manifesting on the trigger, or when the cause is a logic or configuration defect whose manifestation does not depend on timing, ordering, concurrency or capacity; it is absent when the cause names a race, a lock, an ordering, a pause or a capacity condition, and absent when the inputs are silent, since the deterministic share is asked per row and never assumed from Yuan et al.'s 74%, whose transfer to an occurrence model failed verification. [cites:C027] [skill-authored] The **sustaining-loop** modifier is present when `causes[]` names a mechanism that keeps the failure alive once the trigger has passed — request retries, failover contagion, look-aside cache loss amplifying queries, slow error handling, connection-pool imbalance, any loop that consumes the exhausted resource — and absent otherwise; near advertised capacity a loop lets a weak trigger suffice, and the sustaining loop rather than the trigger is the cause to act on. [adapted-from:C032] [adapted-from:C033] [adapted-from:C034] An adversarial trigger is T-rare here, because the threat-model handoff and not this scale assesses the adversary. [skill-authored]

An incident count over a stated window, when the record states the count, the mode it counts and the window's bounds, sets the value on its own and is the only `observed_incident` evidence on this factor: the window ends at its stated end date or, when open-ended, at the date of the record stating it (its incident date when it carries no other); its length is counted in started months and must be at least three; the count is annualised as count times twelve divided by months, rounded to the nearest integer with halves up; the modifiers do not apply to a counted row. [skill-authored] Zero incidents over a stated window of at least twelve months is a count; no count at all is not zero and takes the exposure rule with evidence kind `estimate`. [skill-authored] An availability SLA or an error budget is neither a count nor a measurement of this mode and sets neither gate; a dependency outage is T-rare unless a count says otherwise. [skill-authored] A test result is evidence for the deterministic modifier and for V-excluded, with evidence kind `test_result`; a rating with neither a count nor a test carries `estimate`. [skill-authored]

## Detection, 1 to 10

**How Detection is read.** Detection is the earliest control layer that reliably catches the mode, read off the row's own `controls[]`: the value is the lowest layer value among controls with `kind: detection`, `status: existing`, and `evidence.kind` other than `none`, and *reliably* means exactly that evidence condition and nothing else. [skill-authored] The layers are ordered design review, type system or static check, unit test, integration test, canary or progressive rollout, production alerting, customer report, none; the first four adapt Yuan et al.'s distinction between faults reachable by a static pattern check or code review, by statement-coverage testing of the error-handling path, and by small-scale reproduction, and the last of theirs, log mining, sits at 8. [adapted-from:C028] [adapted-from:C029] Yuan et al.'s percentages are not carried, and because their evidence supports only that a layer *can* catch a mode, the evidence condition is required at every value. [adapted-from:C028] [adapted-from:C029]

Planned controls, prevention controls and compensating controls never move Detection at any value; a circuit breaker, a fallback or a retry budget limits the effect and is rated in Severity, unless the analysis records it with `kind: detection`, in which case it is read by its description like any other. [skill-authored] With no qualifying control the value is 9 or 10 by the user-visibility rule in the table, never lower. [skill-authored] The categorical breaks fall on the band cuts: before code exists (1), from the source without running the system (2 to 3), by running the system before or during limited exposure (4 to 6), in production by the operator's own instrumentation after full exposure (7 to 8), by users or never (9 to 10). [skill-authored]

Description to layer is a lookup on the closed lists in the table for a control recorded with `kind: detection`; a detection control whose description matches no list takes the layer of the nearest listed term and the rationale says which. [skill-authored] A control the inventory describes as a breaker, a fallback, a retry budget or cap, a cache, an idempotency key, a rate limit, a timeout, a signature or schema check at request time, or a validation is prevention or compensating and is not recorded as detection unless the inputs say it alerts, pages or reports. [skill-authored] A control listed in the team's control inventory with no evidence stated takes `evidence.kind: estimate` with the inventory as its reference; `none` is written only when the inputs state the evidence is absent or an incident record shows the control did not catch the mode. [skill-authored]

On a seeded chain the incident's detection narrative governs the evidence of the controls that exist now: a control that acted carries `observed_incident`, a control that existed and did not catch the mode carries `none` on that row, a control added after the incident is planned until the inventory lists it as existing, and when no control caught it the row reads 9 or 10. [skill-authored] A dashboard, log or report a person consulted while the incident was open acted, however late, and carries `observed_incident`; one the record says was not consulted, or could have shown the condition and was not read, carries `none`. [skill-authored] An update-mode re-rating reads Detection from the row's `controls[]` after the control-removed pass and adds no control the row did not carry; an inventory control the row lacks is noted in the update summary for the human re-scorer. [skill-authored] The evidence kind of the Detection rating is the qualifying control's `evidence.kind`, and `estimate` when no control qualifies. [skill-authored] The value 1 mirrors the blocker lint: it is written only with a qualifying design-review control on the row, and every other row is 2 or higher. [skill-authored]

## Lints

Both lints are `machine` rules a script implements from the JSON alone, each stated as a condition over the fields of `schemas/fmea.schema.json`; `quality-and-lint.md` carries the same two ids. [skill-authored]

- `occurrence-estimate-without-trigger` (`machine`, warning): fires on a chain when `ratings.O.evidence_kind` equals `"estimate"`, `ratings.O.value` is greater than or equal to 7, and the chain's `trigger` is absent or equals the empty string. [skill-authored] The Occurrence anchors cap an estimate with no named trigger at 6, so this lint never fires on a rating they produce; it catches converted and hand-edited ratings. [skill-authored]
- `detection-1-without-evidenced-control` (`machine`, blocker): fires on a chain when `ratings.D.value` equals 1 and no entry of `controls[]` has `kind` equal to `"detection"`, `status` equal to `"existing"`, and `evidence.kind` not equal to `"none"`. [skill-authored] The Detection anchor for 1 requires exactly such a control on the row, so the lint is the machine form of that anchor's 1-or-2 step. [skill-authored]

## Priority

Priority is computed by `scripts/priority.ts` only, never in prose: a chain's `priority` from its `ratings` and the loaded table, and its `post_priority`, where `post_ratings` is present, from `post_ratings` with the same table; the report's sort and the computed summary read `priority` alone. [skill-authored]

The shipped table is `data/priority-fmea-software-v1.json`, the skill's own. [skill-authored] It carries an `id`; a `vocabulary` ordered highest priority first, `H`, `M`, `L`, whose order is the only ordering every comparison here, the §10 tests and the report sort use, including for a user-supplied table; `bands` for each of S, O and D, five per factor, indexed 1 to 5 in increasing rating order at the cuts 1, 2 to 3, 4 to 6, 7 to 8, 9 to 10; and a cell for every one of the 125 band combinations. [skill-authored] A rating is read by finding the band that contains its value, and the three band indices select the cell; the cell map lives in that file and no cell value is restated here. [skill-authored] The three letters are the plugin's own label set: no cell or rule of the AIAG-VDA handbook is carried and no band boundary is taken from it (the S split matches, as the negative results state), and the handbook's own Action Priority likewise resolved to three values after its June 2020 errata. [paraphrased:C062] [skill-authored]

The shipped table satisfies the properties the §10 unit tests enforce: priority never decreases as any of S, O or D increases; S of 9 or 10 is never below `M`; S of 1 is always `L`; and severity outranks, as a weak inequality, so that for band indices a greater than b and any c, the cell at (S a, O b, D c) is at least the cell at (S b, O a, D c) and the cell at (S a, O c, D b) is at least the cell at (S b, O c, D a) in the vocabulary order. [skill-authored] RPN is S times O times D, stored in `priority.rpn` for reference and for comparison with legacy sheets, and never used to rank. [skill-authored]

The anchors above are written so that the categorical breaks of every factor fall on the shipped table's band cuts, 1|2, 3|4, 6|7 and 8|9, and the steps inside a band are gradations of the same evidence; two runs may differ inside a band without moving a priority count, and the run-to-run stability claim is made for the shipped bands. [skill-authored] A second table arrives only through `--table-file`, is read from no other location, and is refused only when its shape fails: an id, an ordered vocabulary, bands per factor that tile 1 to 10 without gap or overlap, and a cell for every band combination. [skill-authored] It is then checked against the four properties above and never refused for them: `priority.ts` prints one `warning priority-table-property:` line for each property the table breaks, naming every cell that breaks it, and `validate.ts` records the same finding as a `priority-table-property` warning in `computed.lints`, so the report lists it. [skill-authored] On such a table the properties read the table's own terms: `M` is the vocabulary's second-lowest value and `L` its lowest, S of 9 or 10 and S of 1 are the cells of the S bands that hold those ratings, and the two severity inequalities run over the band indices that S and the other factor both have. [skill-authored] A licensed table that departs from the properties above still drops in, with a warning for each property it breaks, and under it every anchor stays meaningful because each of the ten values is defined on its own, while the stability claim then rests on where that table's cuts fall. [skill-authored] The vocabulary is read from the loaded table and never hard-coded, because priority vocabularies are catalog-scoped in practice: one commercial FMEA tool ships extended and OEM-specific priority catalogs alongside the three-value one and can render the three values as numbers. [paraphrased:C122]

## The review gate

Every rating the skill writes is provisional: it carries `review.status: provisional` until a named person re-scores it against these anchors, one rating at a time, and only then does it read `rescored`. [skill-authored] A re-score that changes a value changes the chain's RPN and may change its priority, so `priority.ts --write` runs again after it, before `validate.ts --write` and the re-render; until it does, `validate.ts` refuses the document with `PRIORITY_MISMATCH`. [skill-authored] In a primary study of LLM-assigned S, O and D ratings, the model did not simply assign uniformly higher scores but reordered the failure modes against the expert ranking. [paraphrased:C067] The gate is therefore a per-rating re-scoring and not a correction factor applied to a score, because a reordering is not the kind of divergence a scale factor repairs. [skill-authored] That study is off-domain — a radiation-oncology FMEA — so it justifies the gate and supports nothing quantitative about software ratings. [cites:C067] [skill-authored] It also found the models' scoring of likelihood, severity and detectability often vague or inconsistent, although the prompts told the models to follow the study's 1 to 10 scale definitions (the prompts were not standardized), and it points both to the subjectivity of manual scoring and to the contextual limits of LLM-based risk estimation. [paraphrased:C069] The rule that every rating carries a rationale against the anchors is the skill's own and is not cited to that study. [skill-authored] The companion rule that every rating carries an evidence kind answers a second finding: an AI-driven FMEA framework generated suggested S, O and D values with no quantitative validation of those values against experts or ground truth. [paraphrased:C071]

## Versioning

The line `scales version: N` at the top of this file is the single declaration of the scales version, and any change to an anchor bumps N. [skill-authored] `meta.scales.version` in an analysis document is the only pin, set from that line when the ratings are made; rows carry no scales version of their own. [skill-authored] When the update mode finds the pin behind N, it marks every chain that has `ratings` as stale with reason `scales-version` (subject to the §5 precedence when another stale rule also fires on the row) and `since_version` set to the new `meta.version`, then advances the pin in the same run. [skill-authored] Because the pin is advanced in the same run, a later update run finds no version gap and adds no further `scales-version` flags, while a row still flagged from the earlier run keeps its flag and its `since_version`. [skill-authored] Rows stay flagged until they are re-scored: the flag clears only when all three of the row's ratings carry `review.status: rescored`, and the re-rating of a `scales-version` row reads all three factors against the new anchors, while any other stale reason carries forward as the common rules above state. [skill-authored]
